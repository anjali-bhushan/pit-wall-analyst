      How it works

      From raw race data to an AI analyst, in four steps.

      1. Data.
      2. BigQuery SQL. The two tables are loaded into BigQuery; SQL builds the standings, team race-craft, pit-lane and pole statistics (bigquery.sql, steps 1–4).
      3. BigQuery ML. A logistic-regression model predicts a podium from four inputs known before the race: starting position (log scale), qualifying gap to pole, the driver's points in his last 3 races and the team's points per car in its last 3 races. It is trained on 2018–2024 and tested only on 2025–2026 (steps 5–9). The inputs were chosen using 2023–2024 as a validation window, so the test seasons were used once, at the end.
      4. Gemini race engineer. The computed tables are passed to Gemini with instructions to answer only from them, cite the numbers and say when the data cannot answer. Facts are computed by SQL; the AI only explains them.

      Limitations

      • The model does not know about weather, penalties, safety cars or crashes, which decide many podiums.
      • Its gain over the simple grid rule is modest; most of the information is in the starting grid.
      • Pit-lane time includes the drive through the pit lane, so it differs by circuit and is not the stationary stop time.
      • Data is a snapshot (see the date in the footer), not live.
